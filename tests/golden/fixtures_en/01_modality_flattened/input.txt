Raw measurements can be unreliable or noisy, and a single reading may point in the wrong direction. Teams that treat one number as a verdict tend to overcorrect. The safer move is to collect a second reading before acting, which usually costs less than the correction would. In our own logs, roughly a third of the alerts that looked urgent on first read turned out to be sampling artifacts. That does not mean alerts should be ignored. It suggests that the cost of a five-minute confirmation is small next to the cost of a wrong rollback.
